CueCraft inline hook rail - option 1

Anchored card rail inside the normal editor

The colorful hook cards sit in the editor's left whitespace and stay vertically anchored to their section headings. CodeMirror can render this as an overlay layer keyed to parsed heading lines, while the Markdown remains untouched.

Best fit Highest recall value. Costs the most horizontal space, but makes the hook rail feel native to the note.

CueCraft inline hook rail - option 2

Collapsed color tabs with one expanded peek

The editor keeps almost all of its normal width. Each generated hook becomes a colored tab in a narrow CodeMirror-adjacent lane; the current section expands into a readable card only when active, hovered, or keyboard focused.

Best fit Safest daily-writing default. Lower visual drama, but the rail is always present without crowding the note.

CueCraft inline hook rail - option 3

Threaded margin notes for calmer reading

This version borrows the hook rail's color language but converts cards into smaller margin notes connected by a vertical thread. It feels less like flashcards and more like a high-signal outline stitched to the live note.

Best fit Great for long professional notes where full hook cards would feel loud. Less memorable than the big rail.

CueCraft inline hook rail - option 4

Active-section composer for writing mode

Instead of showing every hook at full strength, the rail behaves like a live companion to the section under the cursor. As the user writes a new heading and bullets, a draft hook card appears beside that region and resolves into a stable card after generation.

Best fit Most directly solves "see new hooks as I write." Needs careful throttling so the rail feels responsive, not jumpy.

CueCraft inline hook rail - option 5

Hook minimap with focused popout

The colorful rail becomes a scroll minimap for the note. Every hook is a colored block scaled to section height; the currently visible section pops out as a full hook card and points back to the CodeMirror heading.

Best fit Best navigation model for dense notes. Strong spatial memory, but weaker for immediate active recall than full cards.
